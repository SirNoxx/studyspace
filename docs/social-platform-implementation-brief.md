# Studyspace social platform — implementation brief

Expand Studyspace into a cohesive study community where users can publish their work, find people studying similar topics, collaborate on shared collections, and communicate privately. Deliver complete user flows, persistent data, enforced permissions, and operational readiness. Preserve the privacy and reliability of existing personal workspaces.

1. **Profiles and identity.** Make the avatar in the bottom-left navigation open the user's profile rather than Settings. Retain a separate Settings entry. Provide stable profile URLs, an avatar, public username, editable biography, study interests, and optional social links with user-defined labels. Validate links and render them safely. Let users choose whether their profile is public and whether they accept message requests. Existing accounts should retain their current privacy until they explicitly enable public visibility.

2. **Published work and statistics.** Show the author's published collections and notes on their profile, with category filters and links to each publication. Give the account owner a private dashboard showing collection, folder, note, publication, and downloaded-copy counts, plus engagement on their publications. Distinguish downloads of the author's work from publications the author has saved. Define whether metrics count unique users or total events and prevent duplicate retries from inflating them. Public visitors see only public work and explicitly public statistics; private workspace totals, saved downloads, private comments, and draft activity remain private.

3. **Shared collections.** Allow collection owners to invite collaborators and manage inherited collection permissions. Use Owner, Editor, and Viewer roles: Owners manage membership and permissions; Editors create, edit, move, and remove folders and files; Viewers read permitted content. Require invitation acceptance, allow revocation and leaving a collection, and prevent editors from granting access or changing ownership. Restrict ownership transfer to an explicit accepted flow. Define how moving content across permission boundaries works and require a scope preview before it exposes private content. Sharing is distinct from publishing.

4. **Reliable collaboration.** Enforce authorization on every read, mutation, attachment request, and realtime subscription in the database and server. A revoked collaborator must lose access promptly, including access through cached links. Handle concurrent edits without silently overwriting content; retain versions, support recovery, and provide clear conflict resolution. Use transactions for folder moves and multi-record changes. Maintain an audit trail of membership, permission, and destructive changes. Specify offline behavior and how reconnecting clients reconcile changes before implementation.

5. **Discovery and study activity.** Build a unified Discover experience with research publications, study ideas, and requests to study together. Each post should have a clear type, author, category, optional topic tags, publication time, and relevant engagement. Support search, category filtering, and Latest, Following, and Interests feeds. Show only activity users have deliberately made public. Let users follow people and topics, save published work, and visit profiles from posts. Add commenting with editing, deletion, pagination, reporting, and moderation. Clearly distinguish a published snapshot from a shared editable collection.

6. **Private messaging.** Add a Messages tab directly above Inbox. Keep system notifications in Inbox and conversations in Messages. Allow a user to request a conversation with a public-profile user who accepts requests. Display pending requests separately from accepted conversations. Before acceptance, permit one introductory request and prevent repeated unsolicited messages. Recipients can accept, decline, ignore, block, or report. Persist accepted conversations, support unread counts and delivery/retry states, paginate history, and avoid duplicate messages during reconnects. Turning off requests stops new requests without deleting existing conversations. Only participants can read a conversation.

7. **Privacy and community controls.** Provide clear visibility controls, blocking, reporting, rate limits, spam prevention, and moderation tools. Account deletion must have defined behavior for messages, publications, shared ownership, and retained audit records. Sanitize user-generated content and validate uploads. Prevent blocked users from initiating contact or collaboration invitations. Notification settings should separately control messages, invitations, comments, and followed activity.

8. **Engineering delivery.** Begin with an audit of existing authentication, profiles, publications, copies, comments, notifications, and workspace storage. Reuse suitable foundations. Produce an architecture and data model, additive database migrations, indexes, row-level policies, API contracts, permission tests, and a migration/backfill strategy. Include feature flags, rollback instructions, monitoring, actionable errors, keyboard accessibility, responsive layouts, and loading, empty, offline, and failure states. Never describe a mocked or local-only interaction as a completed production integration.

9. **Release criteria.** Ship in stages: profiles and privacy; publishing and discovery; permissioned shared collections; messaging and community controls. Gate each stage on integration and browser tests using multiple accounts. Verify private-data isolation, role enforcement, revocation, cross-account access attempts, invitation states, concurrent changes, message-request restrictions, blocking, retries, and deletion. Establish measurable performance targets for feed pagination, chat delivery, and large collections, then verify them with load tests. Document completed behavior, remaining limitations, operational ownership, and release evidence before general availability.
